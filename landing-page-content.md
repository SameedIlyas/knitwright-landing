# Knitwright — landing page content brief

The messaging source of truth for the public site: what Knitwright is, who it is for, why
a brand should choose it, and how a brand gets from "I have an idea" to "the boxes
arrived". Every section below has **paste-ready copy** plus a short note on what the
section is for.

- **This doc is the content.** `docs/landing-page-prompt.md` is the *build* brief (visual
  identity, components, motion, accessibility). Use both: take the words from here and
  the look from there.
- **Name.** The product ships as **Knitwright** (`app/layout.tsx`, studio shell, quote
  panel). Older docs, the PRD and `landing-page-prompt.md` still say "Kitwright". Decide
  on one and find-and-replace across the others before you build.
- **Status.** Knitwright is pre-launch. The page is an **early-access waitlist**, not a
  sign-up flow. Nothing on it may imply live customers, volume, or results we don't have.

## 1. Who we serve

### Primary — the brand founder (the buyer)

Founder of an emerging performance, activewear, or team-kit brand, based mainly in North
America, the UK, the EU and Australia, producing roughly **$100k–$2M a year**.

- **Strong at:** brand, audience, demand, design taste.
- **Weak at:** apparel engineering and production. Has no technical designer on staff.
- **What they want:** a made product, on time, that matches what they approved. Outcomes
  and certainty, not another tool to learn.
- **What keeps them up:** a factory that ghosts them after the deposit; a bulk run that
  doesn't match the sample; cash tied up in goods they can't sell.

> **Copy — "Built for"**
> **Built for brands that are good at everything except the factory.**
> You have the designs, the audience, and the orders. Knitwright is the production team
> you haven't hired yet.

### Secondary — the product person (the daily user)

The designer or product lead who lives in specs, measurements and samples. Wants precise
control of the numbers without a PLM system's weight.

> **Copy**
> **For the person who reads the measurement table.** Every point of measurement,
> grade and tolerance is in the spec, editable, and moves the pattern, the flat and the
> 3D garment with it.

### Segments to call out on the page

| Segment | What they make | Why Knitwright fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Performance and training brands** | Training tees, running tops, base layers | Technical fabrics and tolerances matter; the spec engineering is the hard part |
| **Activewear labels** | Everyday athletic tops, lifestyle-athletic | Small first runs, fast iteration, clean reorders |
| **Team kit and club uniforms** | Club tees, warm-up tops, polos, sublimated kit | Repeat orders every season, logos and panel colours, the same spec every time |
| **Creator and community brands** | Merch that has to feel like real product | No production knowledge, high quality bar, needs someone accountable |

### Who it is *not* for (say this — it builds trust)

- Enterprise brands that already run a PLM and a sourcing team.
- Anyone looking for a **factory directory** or wanting to choose and manage their own
  factory.
- Consumer or print-on-demand one-offs. Knitwright is a production backend for brands,
  not a storefront.

> **Copy**
> **Not a marketplace. Not a directory. Not print-on-demand.** If you want to pick and
> manage your own factory, we're not the right fit — and you can still take your tech
> pack with you.

## 2. The problem

**Section purpose:** make the reader nod. Describe the industry status quo — never
present these as numbers measured by Knitwright.

> **`01 / THE STATUS QUO`**
> ## Small brands can design. They can't produce.
>
> **Months** — to find a factory that answers, samples honestly, and doesn't disappear
> once you're past the deposit.
>
> **Rounds** — weeks per sample round, and the spec drifts a little every time someone
> re-types it into a new spreadsheet.
>
> **Last** — you find out about quality when the boxes land, after the money has gone.
>
> The hard part was never the design. It's the engineering of the spec, and the
> execution behind it.

### The pains, in the founder's own words (for body copy, ads and FAQ)

- "I sent the same tech pack to four factories and got four different garments."
- "I don't know what a correct seam allowance is, so I can't tell when it's wrong."
- "The sample was perfect. The bulk wasn't."
- "I paid 50% up front and then they stopped replying."
- "Reordering means explaining the whole thing again to someone new."

### Why the existing options don't fix it

| If you use… | You get… | But… |
|---|---|---|
| **An AI tech-pack generator** | A drafted spec in minutes | Nobody checks it, and it stops at the document |
| **A tech-pack or PLM tool** | Good documentation and collaboration | You still have to find, vet and manage a factory yourself |
| **A sourcing marketplace** | An introduction to a factory | You manage the relationship and carry the quality risk |
| **An agent or freelancer** | Someone who knows factories | Opaque pricing, one person's bandwidth, no system |
| **Enterprise PLM / ERP** | Everything | Built for 200-person brands, priced and staffed for them |

> **Copy**
> **Everyone owns one slice. The handoffs are where brands lose months.**

## 3. What we do

**Section purpose:** state the offer plainly. Four things, combined.

> **`WHAT KNITWRIGHT DOES`**
> ## One product, from the first sketch to the last carton.

1. **We draft the spec.**
   Upload a photo, sketch, competitor product or moodboard. Knitwright drafts the complete
   technical specification: annotated technical flats, a graded measurement table with
   tolerances, construction notes, fabric and trims.

2. **A named technician verifies it.**
   A technician in our studio checks what software can't be trusted to — seam
   allowances, grading logic, buildability — and signs it by name, with a date.

3. **We make it.**
   Production runs on lines we own and manage. You never see, choose, or contact a
   factory. You see *Knitwright Verified Production*: one quality tier, a price, a lead
   time, and a guarantee.

4. **We ship it — and you release the money.**
   Quote to delivery in one place: instant costing, sample or straight to bulk, inline
   QC, milestone escrow you release on QC pass, and shipping to your door.

> **Copy — short version for the hero support line**
> Knitwright turns your design into a verified, factory-ready specification, then
> manufactures it and ships it to you. A named technician signs off every spec. You never
> source, vet, or chase a factory.

## 4. How it works (the journey)

**Section purpose:** show the path is short and that the brand does very little.

> **`02 / THE FLOW`**
> ## Four steps. You do two of them.

| # | Step | Who | Copy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Upload what you have** | `YOU` | A photo, a sketch, a competitor's product, a moodboard. Two answers change the spec — category and quantity. Everything else starts correct by default. |
| 2 | **Get a drafted spec** | `KNITWRIGHT` | Technical flats with measurement callouts, a graded size run with tolerances, construction notes, fabric and trims. Change anything by just saying it. |
| 3 | **A technician verifies it** | `KNITWRIGHT` | Named, in our studio. They check seam allowances, grading and buildability, then sign it. |
| 4 | **Approve, and we make it** | `YOU` | Sample first or straight to bulk. Inline QC, escrow, shipped to your door. |

### The full route, idea to reorder (for a "What happens after I sign up" section)

```
IDEA ─▶ INTAKE ─▶ DRAFT SPEC ─▶ STUDIO SIGN-OFF ─▶ SAMPLE ─▶ QUOTE & ORDER ─▶ PRODUCTION & QC ─▶ SHIPPED ─▶ REORDER
 you     you        us             us             us+you      you              us                  us        you (1 click)
```

1. **Intake** — upload references, answer category and quantity. Fit, fabric weight,
   target price and deadline are there if you want them, folded away if you don't.
2. **Draft spec** — the tech pack appears: flats, graded measurements, construction,
   materials. Refine it in plain language ("raglan, long sleeves, cobalt body").
3. **Studio sign-off** — a technician reviews and signs. The spec is marked
   *Verified for production* with their name and the date.
4. **Sample** — we make a sample. It comes back **measured against your spec's own
   tolerances**, with photos you can pin comments on. Approve it, or request changes.
5. **Quote and order** — instant per-unit costing with the breakdown shown. Pick a
   quantity tier, see the lead time, pay a deposit into escrow.
6. **Production and QC** — track the stage you're in and the one after it. Inline QC
   against an AQL standard, with photos.
7. **Release and ship** — the balance stays in escrow until QC passes and **you**
   release it. Then it ships.
8. **Reorder** — same spec, same line, same price band. One click.

## 5. What you get (the product, feature by feature)

**Section purpose:** give the product person the depth they're looking for. Use as a
features grid, a "Look inside" tabbed section, or on a separate /product page.

Tags: no tag = in the product today · **`ROADMAP`** = planned, say "coming" on the page.

### The tech pack — the spec is the contract
- **Annotated technical flats** — front and back line drawings with measurement callouts
  and numbered construction markers.
- **Graded measurement table** — every point of measurement across the size run
  (XS–XXL) with its tolerance. Grading is calculated from the spec, not typed.
- **Edit the numbers directly** — the table is an input: change a measurement and the
  flat, the 3D garment and the pattern pieces follow.
- **Construction and seam notes** tied to markers on the flat.
- **Fabric and trims** — the materials block, linked to your materials library.
- **Verification status on every version** — *Verified for production* with the
  reviewer's name and date, or *Unverified draft* when the spec has changed since
  sign-off.
- **Export a real tech pack document** — front, back and detail views, the graded table
  with tolerances, fabric and construction, and the approval line.
- **Export flat pattern pieces as DXF** — front, back and sleeve pieces drafted from your
  spec's own measurements, ready to open in pattern and cutting software. Any value that
  comes from a standard drafting rule rather than your spec is labelled as an assumption.

### The design assistant and configurator
- **Describe the change, see it happen** — "raglan with long sleeves, cobalt body" updates
  the flat, the 3D garment and the measurement table together.
- **It tells you what can't be built, and why** — ask for a polo with cap sleeves and it
  explains that polos come in short and three-quarter sleeves, from the same style rules
  production uses.
- **Styles today:** crew, V-neck, raglan, polo, henley.
- **Options:** cap, short, three-quarter and long sleeves (per style) · cropped, regular
  or long body · athletic or relaxed fit.
- **Colour by panel** — body, sleeves, collar and placket, in any colour.
- **All-over print** and **logo upload** that carry through to the flat and the render.
- **Live 3D garment** that answers to the spec, not a static model.
- **Photoreal renders** from the technical flat, for decks and pre-sales. *(Uses an
  external image service.)*

### Samples
- Sample rounds tracked per spec version.
- **Measured against your tolerances** — each point is marked pass or flag
  automatically, e.g. a sleeve at 21.8 against a 21.0 ±0.5 spec is flagged.
- Photo review with pinned comments; approve to release bulk, or request changes.

### Quote and order
- **Instant costing that shows its arithmetic** — materials and trims, make, finishing and
  pack, QA and spec, platform fee. The lines always add up to the price you pay.
- **Quantity tiers** with the unit price and lead time at each break.
- **One production tier** — Knitwright Verified Production. No factory list, no
  selection.
- Sample first, or straight to bulk.

### Production
- Stage tracker: sample, sample approved, bulk, final QC, shipped.
- Inline QC snapshot against an AQL standard, with photos.
- **Milestone escrow** — what's paid, what's held, released by you on QC pass.
- Issue handling with money back on verified defects.

### Everything around it
- **Projects** — every style, its stage, and its next action. Duplicate a style to start
  a variant.
- **One-click reorder** of any shipped style.
- **Materials library** — fabrics, trims, labels and packaging, reused across styles so
  repeat orders stay consistent.
- **Messages** — threads with the studio, scoped to a project or a single measurement.
- **Brand settings** — logo, labels and size standard.
- **Product passport** — composition, origin and care per product, structured for the EU
  Digital Product Passport. **`ROADMAP`** for full regulatory readiness.
- **Team roles, Shopify and storefront export, analytics** — **`ROADMAP`**.

## 6. Why choose Knitwright

> **`WHY KNITWRIGHT`**
> ## Other tools hand you a document. We hand you a finished product.

### 1. A named human signs every spec
AI drafting a tech pack is becoming cheap — and a confidently wrong seam allowance is
something a founder can't catch. Every Knitwright spec is verified by a technician in our
studio, by name, with a date. **That sign-off is what you're buying.**

*Proof on the page:* the two-state verification card — *Verified for production · Adeel R.
· Studio · 2 days ago* next to *Unverified draft · Spec changed since sign-off.* The badge

### 2. There is no factory to manage
You never source, vet, brief, chase or switch a factory. You see one production tier with

then on the quality risk is yours. We don't run a directory, so we stay accountable for
the result.

### 3. The spec is one source of truth
The flat, the 3D garment, the graded measurement table and the pattern pieces are four
views of one spec. Change the sleeve and every number moves with it. There's no second

### 4. Your money waits for you
Deposit into escrow at order. The balance is held until QC passes and **you** release it

### 5. Built for sportswear, not all of fashion
Style rules, fit, grading and tolerances are tuned to performance and athletic tops:
crew, V-neck, raglan, polo, henley. The assistant refuses what can't be built, because it
checks against the same rules production uses.

### 6. Your designs stay yours
Your specs are never shown to another brand. Export the full tech pack and DXF pattern

## 11. Messaging rules

### Voice
Plain, active, end-user language. Short declarative sentences. Buttons say what happens.
Confidence comes from specifics (a name, a tolerance, a line item), not adjectives.

### Say / don't say

| Say | Don't say |
|---|---|
| verified spec, signed by a technician | AI-powered, revolutionary, game-changing |
| we make it, we ship it | seamless, effortless, magic |
| Knitwright Verified Production | our partner factories, our network of 500 factories |
| production we own and run | marketplace, directory, sourcing platform |
| you release the money | we guarantee everything |
| measured against your tolerances | perfect quality |
| early access, first group of brands | trusted by hundreds of brands |

No exclamation marks. No emoji as icons.

### Claims we can make
- Every spec is verified and signed by a named technician before production.
- Brands never see, choose, or contact a factory.
- Escrow balance is released by the brand on QC pass.
- Costing is itemised and the lines sum to the unit price.
- Specs, tech packs and DXF pattern pieces are exportable and owned by the brand.
- The flat, 3D garment, measurement table and pattern are driven by one spec.

### Claims we must not make (until they're true and sourced)
- Any customer count, logo, testimonial, review, or case study.
- Any measured result ("40% fewer sample rounds", "ships in 14 days").
- Any funding, press or award badge.
- Specific pricing, minimums, lead times or a launch date — use the placeholders.
- Numbers from the demo fixtures (e.g. $12.60/unit, 18 days) **unless** clearly labelled
  illustrative.
